# Agent Gateway decisions — `AGW-D*`

Decisions arising from [`design.md`](design.md). This is the Java Maven Agent Gateway component co-deployed with DP v1, not a separate app/process and distinct from the host-local Go agent. **All are `PROPOSED`.** None is ratified. **There is no gateway code, no `.proto`, and none may be written** — open item **AG-1** / open item **6** (the agent↔plane wire and state machine) is the architect group's to close, and this family is the plane-side *input* to closing it.

**ID discipline (`I3`):** IDs are immutable and appended, never renumbered. The family is registered in [`../../README.md`](../../README.md), [`../../DECISIONS.md`](../../DECISIONS.md) (family table and ratification matrix) and the root `CLAUDE.md`. `AGW-D*` does **not** re-decide `D*`, `B/D*`, `SM-D*`, `UI-D*`, `DM-D*`, `FE-D*`, `AG-D*` or `BE-D*` — cross-cutting and sibling choices are cited, not restated. To change a decision, add one that supersedes it.

**Scope of the family, fixed at registration:** gateway/module boundaries · mTLS enrollment relay and revocation handling · agent session and lease ownership · command delivery and acknowledgement · backpressure · reconnect/replay · HA routing · telemetry ingest boundaries · the future scale-out extraction path. **Nothing agent-internal.** Existing `AG-D*` decisions remain agent-owned; every dependency or conflict with one is recorded in the table below, and `AGW-D*` never silently amends an `AG-D*`.

> **Amendment policy for this draft.** The decisions drafted 2026-09-08 (starting at `AGW-D1`) are a first draft with no code and no ratification. Until the family is built against or ratified, review corrections in the same cycle may be applied in place and itemized in the changelog. Post-ratification the supersession rule applies normally.

⚠️ **This family rests on an unratified base** — `SM-D24`/`SM-D28` (session and authority at the DP), `BE-D1`/`BE-D3`/`BE-D12`/`BE-D13` (record of truth, module layout, apply engine and its tables), `AG-D4`/`AG-D5`/`AG-D9` (journal, outcomes, buffer). **Ratification position: after the `BE-D*` and `AG-D*` families.** Two decisions are proposals against proposals and carry their own ordering: `AGW-D1` after `BE-D3`; `AGW-D7` after `SM-D28`. **Two more carry a ratification *gate* rather than an ordering, and cannot be ratified with the family until it clears:** `AGW-D15` needs the CP issuance contract (design §13 AGW-18 — without it the lease ledger's timeout recovery has no form that preserves the single-use nonce), and `AGW-D16` needs design §13 AGW-17 (runtime verification of reported replica identities, or explicit acceptance of the grammar-bounded residual) **and** design §13 AGW-20 (the report-intake contract its ordering, watermark and liveness clauses consume), with its retirement clauses additionally needing design §13 AGW-19. **`AGW-D15` and `AGW-D16` are independently gated; their mechanism sketch — [`lease-and-registry-sketch.md`](lease-and-registry-sketch.md) — is not part of the `AGW-D1`…`AGW-D14` set's ratification.**

## `AGW-D1` — `agent-gateway` is a fifth plane module, co-deployed in v1 (one replica), independently deployable by construction

The backend gains one module, `agent-gateway` (Maven module `agent-gateway`, package root `com.solace.sm.agw..`), owning agent-facing gRPC/mTLS sessions, credits, reconnects and replica claims. In v1 it ships inside the single on-prem Data Plane deployable beside `dp-core` and `mesh-bridge`; because that deployable is one process and `dp-core` is single-instance, **v1 runs exactly one gateway replica**. The module must be buildable as its own deployable without code change (design §2.3, §2.5) — that is where a second replica comes from. This **amends `BE-D3`**: the plane-module count becomes five — `cp-core`, `dp-core`, `console-gateway`, `mesh-bridge`, `agent-gateway`. Nothing else in `BE-D3` changes: `I7` holds, `console-gateway` still composes both planes' service interfaces, `mesh-bridge` still holds the only broker session.

**What "count" is amended.** `BE-D3` counts *plane modules*, not Maven artifacts — the reactor already carries seven (`backend/pom.xml`: `cp-api`, `dp-api`, `mesh-events`, `cp-core`, `dp-core`, `console-gateway`, `arch-tests`) and no `mesh-bridge` artifact because `BE-D3` packages it inside `dp-core`. `AGW-D1` adds one plane module *and* one Maven module. `architecture.md` §3.1's "two backend modules" was already stale under `BE-D3` and is now stale by two — an impact notice, not an edit (design §11).

**Rationale.** The alternative — an agent listener package inside `dp-core` — is the shape the brief exists to prevent: a gRPC server that can reach repositories directly will, under schedule pressure, and its later extraction becomes the rewrite. A separate module with build-enforced imports (`AGW-D2`) costs one pom and a handful of ArchUnit rules today and buys an extraction that is an adapter swap. The console-gateway precedent (`BE-D3`) shows the reactor already tolerates a module that depends on `*-api` only.

**Rejected.** *A `dp-app` deployable* — no such artifact exists in the baseline and `BE-D3` says on-prem is one deployable; inventing a name for the deployable would cite something undefined. *Placing the gateway in the CP* — that is what `B/D16` literally says and what `SM-D24` re-homes; this decision follows `SM-D24` and flags it (conflicts table), it does not pick. *Claiming multi-replica in v1* — the deployable is one process; saying otherwise would be the "fake horizontal scalability" the brief names.

**Ordering.** Ratifies after `BE-D3`, never in the same round.

## `AGW-D2` — The boundary: `dp-core` owns state and writes; the gateway owns sessions and nothing durable

`dp-core` owns all durable Data Plane business state and **every write to `sm_data`**, including the eleven tables this design adds — six in design §5.2 (`agent_fleet_epoch`, `agent_identity`, `agent_session`, the `agent_claim` ledger, the `agent_request_tombstone` that keeps `REQUEST_EXPIRED` decidable past retention, and `agent_command`) and five in [`lease-and-registry-sketch.md`](lease-and-registry-sketch.md) (`agent_lease_grant` with its sequence, `agent_lease`, and the replica registry's `agent_replica` / `agent_replica_cap` / `agent_replica_incarnation`, `AGW-D15` and `AGW-D16`). `agent-gateway` owns agent-facing sessions, leases *in relay*, backpressure, command delivery and reconnect handling. It may not import DP repositories, JPA entities or `dp-core` at all; it may not depend on any JDBC/JPA/Flyway/datasource artifact; it may not look up beans from the shared Spring context (constructor injection of ports only); it has no database principal and no broker credential of its own; it may not publish events; it has no inbound network surface but the agent port and a mute readiness socket; it reaches the DP only through the `dp-api` ports (`AGW-D6`). v1 co-deploys both in the Data Plane deployable, but they remain separate modules and independently deployable. **Stated honestly:** because v1 is one process, the *process* holds the DP datasource; this decision guarantees that the *module* cannot reach it, and process-level credential isolation arrives at extraction (`AGW-D14`, design §12 limit 13).

**Enforcing artifacts** (design §2.6): ArchUnit rules `agwUsesDpApiOnly`, `agwNeverImportsCp`, `cpNeverImportsAgw`, `dpNeverImportsAgw`, `consoleGatewayNeverImportsAgw`, `agwNeverImportsConsoleGateway`, `agwNeverImportsEvents`, `eventsNeverImportAgw`, `agwHoldsNoDatasource`, `agwNeverLooksUpBeans`, `onlyAgwTouchesGrpc`, `dpApiPortsCarryNoImplementation`, each with a planted-violation fixture; `AgwDependencyAllowListTest` over the module's *resolved* dependencies. The existing plane rules key on `cp..`/`dp..` only, so a new top-level namespace is unguarded until named in both directions — hence the reverse rules; and import rules alone do not stop an `ApplicationContext.getBean(DataSource.class)` in a shared JVM — hence the lookup rule. **Until those tests exist and their fixtures fire, this boundary is a proposal, not a guarantee** (`L19`).

**Rationale.** Every failure this design is asked to prevent — hidden shared-database coupling, fake horizontal scalability, a gateway that can decide state — is an *import* before it is a behaviour. The repo already enforces `I7` and JCSMP confinement this way with tested rules (`PlaneBoundaryTest`); extending the same mechanism is the cheapest guarantee available, and the only one that survives a hurried Friday.

**Relation to `I7`.** `I7` is cross-plane. This rule is intra-DP. It is deliberately **not** proposed as an `I8` in the synced invariants block: the block is shared across future repos, and a module-specific import rule belongs in the module's design plus `arch-tests`, where it is executable. The root `CLAUDE.md` carries a short boundary statement pointing here.

## `AGW-D3` — Session ownership is a `dp-core`-owned row with a monotonic fencing epoch

One row per agent in `sm_data.agent_session` (design §5.2): `owner_replica_id`, `fencing_epoch`. When an agent's stream arrives and passes admission, the gateway calls `SessionRegistryPort.claim(thumbprint, …)`; `dp-core` executes one statement that share-locks the agent's **unrevoked** `agent_identity` row, then `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE … RETURNING` to set the owner and increment the epoch — a revoked or unknown thumbprint yields zero rows and no claim — and hands the gateway an **`AgentSessionRef{agentId, fencingEpoch}`**: DP-issued, opaque, and carrying **no tenant**. Every subsequent agent-bound write (the delivery poll itself, `pull`, `renew`, `received`, `ack`, `report`, agent-source `ingest`, agent-bound `record`, `heartbeat`) carries that ref; the claim is idempotent through an **insert-only ledger** (`agent_claim`, keyed by `requestId` with a replica + certificate fingerprint): a retry returns its **own** original epoch and whether it is still current, never a new claim; a reused id with a different fingerprint is refused; only a genuinely new request touches the session row, where owner and epoch move together — a delayed retry of a superseded claim can neither seize ownership nor mint an epoch; `dp-core` derives the tenant from `agent_id` — **globally unique by `UNIQUE (agent_id)` on `agent_identity`**, as `command_id` is by `UNIQUE (command_id)` — and the write statement **share-locks the session row and joins on `fencing_epoch = $epoch`**. A stale replica's write affects zero rows and is classified `STALE_EPOCH`; a claim committing concurrently serializes against it instead of racing it; and a remote adapter can implement the identical write with nothing but the ref and the ids, because the port asks for nothing else. Ownership changes **only** by a new claim, which happens only because the agent connected elsewhere. There is no reaper.

**Enforcing artifacts:** the claim CTE (single arbiter = PK; identity `FOR SHARE`); the fenced-write CTE shape deriving the tenant by join; the two global `UNIQUE` constraints; an `agent_session_epoch_only_rises` trigger; column-scoped runtime `UPDATE` grants on `agent_session`; the `agent_claim` ledger whose fingerprint covers every claim input; tests `SplitBrainFencedIT` (asserting the two-poller case and all eight fenced writes), `FailoverRedeliversOnceIT`, `ClaimRefusesRevokedContract`, `ClaimLedgerContract`, `LostReplyRetryContract`. The DDL is a **sketch that has been executed but not implemented** (S11/F5): among design §5.2's obligations (a)–(l), (n), (p) and (r), every SQL-backed executable leg is measured green in [`evidence/pg14-execution.md`](evidence/pg14-execution.md) (`8551e5c`, `PostgreSQL 14.24 (Debian 14.24-1.pgdg13+2)`, 224 legs across both documents, 0 `FAIL`), while **(p) is `N/A-engine`** because its classification is written as SQL nowhere here and its durable half is design §13 AGW-20. The Java tests above are **named, not written**; no product code exists; this decision stays `PROPOSED`. ((m), (o), (q) and (s) are retired: they named artifacts that moved to [`lease-and-registry-sketch.md`](lease-and-registry-sketch.md) and reappear there among its own obligations, (L1) onward; the letters are not reused.)

**Rationale.** The alternatives were run through the split-brain scenario (design §5.3): *sticky load-balancer routing with no record* lets two replicas both believe they own an agent after a partition and both deliver the same command — undetectable, because nothing records who is right. *Gateway-side coordination (etcd/Redis/Hazelcast)* solves it but makes the gateway own durable coordination state, contradicting `AGW-D2`, and adds an infrastructure dependency the baseline does not have. A row in the database the DP already owns, with the epoch check *inside* the write, is the smallest thing that is both correct and consistent with `BE-D1` ("PostgreSQL rows are the only system of record").

**Rejected.** *Epoch in the lease* — `SM-D28`'s lease is CP-issued and hour-long; ownership changes on every reconnect and must be DP-local and instant. *Checking the epoch before the write* — a pre-check-then-write is exactly the race a partition exploits; the locked join in the statement is the point. *Fencing only the command path* — the first review found telemetry and audit writes unfenced; every agent-bound write is fenced or the split-brain test is a false green. *Ports keyed by `(tenantId, agentId)`* — the Codex review showed the fenced SQL needed a tenant the port signatures did not carry, so a remote adapter could only have supplied it from process-local memory or a caller field; a DP-issued ref plus globally unique ids removes both.

## `AGW-D4` — Command delivery: intent first, notify then pull, at-least-once delivery, epoch-fenced, at-most-once execution

A command exists only as a durable `sm_data.agent_command` row created by `dp-core` after the corresponding `apply_step_intent` row is committed (`BE-D13` rule 0, write-before-act, extended one hop). The row copies `generation` and `idempotency_key` from the intent so the agent's journal (`AG-D4`) and the DP's arbiter key identically. States: `QUEUED → OFFERED → DELIVERED → ACKED | REFUSED | SUPERSEDED`, CHECK-carried, with `SUPERSEDED` reachable from **both** `QUEUED` and `OFFERED` (a never-offered command must be supersedable, or the newer generation cannot be admitted) and a terminal ack accepted from `OFFERED` **or** `DELIVERED` (the delivery ack may be lost). Each row carries `required_capability` (`apply-config` for `preflight`/`apply`, `lifecycle` for `restart`/`stop`, CHECK-matched to the operation). Delivery is `B/D1`-shaped: `CommandFeedPort.pollDeliverable` is **one fenced reservation statement** (design §5.2) that joins the ownership row on the caller's epoch and owner — a stale or non-owning replica stamps and receives nothing — and the agent's durable `agent_lease` row (`AGW-D15`: capabilities, target, bindings, certificate binding, epochs, expiry and the stored ceilings/margin), stamps the commands that row authorizes and returns only those; a notice is sent only after that transaction commits; a command the lease cannot authorize is moved by `dp-core` to `REFUSED(CAPABILITY_MISSING | TARGET_MISMATCH | BINDING_NOT_IN_LEASE)` **before any notice**, a DP authorization outcome never charged to the agent, and with no current lease row nothing is offered; `pull` re-checks the same predicates; the gateway sends `GenerationAvailable` (no payload) only while the lease is `ACTIVE` and `expires_at − now ≥ verb_ceilings[operation] + admission_margin` (the offer gate, mirroring the agent's own admission rule in `go-agent/design.md` §4.3); the agent answers with `CommandPull` when it is ready to journal; the gateway serves `CommandOffer` with the rendered bytes from an idempotent `pull`; the agent's **`CommandReceived`** (its journal's `RECEIVED` step, `AG-D4`) moves the row to `DELIVERED` through `CommandAckPort.received` and **releases nothing** in the apply engine; only the terminal `CommandAck` advances the step. On reclaim by another replica **every `QUEUED`/`OFFERED`/`DELIVERED` command for that agent is re-notified**; the agent's same-key-same-hash rule makes the duplicate a no-op re-report; same-key-different-hash is `REFUSED(KEY_HASH_MISMATCH)` and recorded as a DP protocol error. Operations are `preflight`, `apply`, `restart`, `stop` — no scheduling verb (`B/D14`'s v1 cut). Per binding: ascending generation, one outstanding `apply`, `stop` may interleave (the `BE-D13` stop exemption), `preflight` precedes its `apply`.

**Enforcing artifacts:** the `agent_command` DDL with `UNIQUE (tenant_id, apply_id, intent_id, agent_id)`, `UNIQUE (command_id)`, the triple FK to `apply_step_intent`, and CHECKs `op_known`, `cap_known`, `cap_matches_op`, `state_known` (incl. `DELIVERED`), `outcome_iff_terminal` (`DELIVERED` carries no outcome), `offered_has_epoch` (admitting `QUEUED`/`SUPERSEDED` with a NULL epoch), `delivered_has_time`; the `received` and `ack` statements with their distinct source-state predicates; tests `DuplicateNoticeIsIdempotentIT`, `MidApplyDisconnectResumesIT`, `OfferGateTest`, `CapabilityGateContract`, `DeliveredIsNotTerminalContract`, `SupersedeQueuedCommandContract`.

**Rationale.** At-least-once delivery is the only honest promise a connection-oriented transport can make; pretending otherwise (exactly-once delivery) is the "fake scalability" the brief names. At-most-once *execution* already exists on both ends — `AG-D4`'s journal and `BE-D13`'s intent arbiter — so the gateway's job is to never lose a command (durable row) and never invent one (no notice without a row). Notify-then-pull keeps `B/D1`'s pull shape, keeps payloads out of gateway memory until the agent is ready, and gives a reconnecting agent a natural way to skip bytes it already holds. Splitting the promise this way also keeps `dp-remote` untouched: the Docker adapter never sees `agent_command`.

**Rejected.** *Commands buffered in gateway memory* — lost on death, invisible to the console. *Exactly-once via a distributed transaction between gateway and agent* — no such thing over a network; the journal is the real mechanism. *Push the payload with the notice* — strains `B/D1` and holds rendered secrets in gateway memory for agents that are not ready. *`RECEIVED` as a `CommandAck.outcome`* — the Codex review showed the row's CHECK excluded it while the ack statement would have terminalized it, releasing the apply wait state before the agent acted; delivery and outcome are now two acks with two statements. *Letting the agent be the only capability check* — a `report-only` agent would then refuse a `restart` and the apply path would record an agent failure for what is a DP authorization decision.

## `AGW-D5` — Design the message and state model first; one connection, three RPCs, closed catalogues; the `.proto` is a later labelled draft

The protocol is specified as a **message catalogue** (design §4.3), a **gateway-side session state machine** with an explicit admission step (§4.2), **ordering/duplicate/stale rules** (§4.4) and a **closed error enum** (§4.11), bound to `B/D16`'s transport: one agent-initiated mTLS/HTTP2 connection on 443 carrying `Enroll` (unary, admission-limited), `Connect` (bidi, control) and `Telemetry` (bidi, samples) as separate HTTP/2 streams. Times on the wire are absolute UTC instants used for **display and diagnostics only**; ordering uses generation, `batch_seq` and `sequence`, and the trust label is decided at DP receive time — the agent's clock never enters ordering, trust or admission (`B/D15`). Capabilities and error codes are closed enums. Stream separation is described honestly: it removes application-level head-of-line blocking between control and telemetry, not connection-level flow control or TCP loss (RFC 9113), so outstanding telemetry bytes are capped at half the connection window and TCP loss is a stated limit. **Any `.proto` derived from this is a labelled, unratified draft with no codegen** until open item 6 / `AG-1` closes under governance; this decision fixes the *model* the `.proto` must encode and the evolution rules it must obey (`AGW-D11`).

**Rationale.** The fork branch's surrogate wire (`origin/dev/go-agent:go-agent/docs/p2-wire-assumptions.md`, non-authoritative) and its own audit show what happens when the encoding leads: twenty-two findings, most of them "the plane side never decided this". Deciding the model first — who sends what, when, keyed by what — is the deliverable a `.proto` author needs and the one thing the agent design (`go-agent/design.md` §3–§4, obligations only) explicitly left to the plane.

**Choices that answer the surrogate's open questions** (cited by path, never by that branch's decision numbers): first lease on `SessionAccept`, not `EnrollResponse`; notify-then-pull rather than push (its M3); report trust label DP-derived at receive time, not agent-asserted; `verb_ceilings` and `admission_margin` on `LeaseGrant` (its C5); dual revocation epochs on the wire with plane-side storage (its M5); `Error` is a coded enum (its M7); `ADMISSION_BLOCKED` is an ack detail, not a session state (its M1); separate telemetry stream on the same connection rather than a second connection or credential (its M2, with the HOL limit stated); no blob fetch path; no `StageBarrier`/`RestartWave` messages (design §13 AGW-9); structured `preflight_result` on the ack (its N4).

**Rejected.** *Writing the `.proto` now* — instructed against, and it would present a governance-owned closure as done. *One bidi stream for everything* — application-level head-of-line blocking between a 64 KiB telemetry batch and a `Revoke`. *A second TLS connection for telemetry* — doubles session state, credentials and reconnect surface for a gain HTTP/2 multiplexing gives at the application layer; TCP head-of-line blocking is not defeated by either choice. *A `blob_ref` fetch for large payloads* — a second inbound surface and an `I6` store outside the render call; refused in favour of a stated size limit.

## `AGW-D6` — Typed semantic ports in `dp-api` are the only gateway↔DP contract

Nine ports (design §3.2): `EnrollmentPort`, `AgentIdentityPort`, `SessionRegistryPort`, `LeasePort` (with three distinct operations — `current`, a **read** that returns an unexpired, certificate-bound projection with no CP call and no write; `issue`, the one nonce-free grant, permitted only while **no `agent_lease` row has ever existed** for the identity and arbitrated durably by a partial unique index; and `renew`, every later grant, under `SM-D28`'s single-use nonce), `CommandFeedPort`, `CommandAckPort` (with distinct `received` and `ack` operations), `ActualStatePort` (whose reply returns DP-derived `trust`, orthogonal `disposition`, and the DP-minted replica ids — the stable one reused, the ephemeral one minted per incarnation, `AGW-D16`; persistence is routed as design §13 `AGW-20`), `TelemetryIngestPort` (whose source identity is producer-specific, `AGW-D9`), `AgentAuditPort`. Rules (§3.1): defined in `dp-api`, implemented in `dp-core`, consumed by `agent-gateway`; every operation carries a `requestId` — **derived from the wire `message_id`** for every agent-caused operation **except `ActualStatePort.report`, which derives one `requestId` per item from the DP-resolved `agent_id` and the item's `item_id`** (§3.1 rule 2's `report` exception) — so a wire retry and a port retry are one request and the gateway keeps no durable state to remember ids — and is idempotent under it **by a durable arbiter, never by a code path**: enrolment keyed by `(token, sha256(CSR))` so a lost reply is recoverable (and the stored reply is public material only, so a replay by someone holding the bytes but not the key yields a certificate they cannot use); `claim` by the insert-only `agent_claim` ledger, whose fingerprint covers **every** input; `issue` and `renew` by the `agent_lease_grant` **request/outcome ledger, reserved before the CP is called** so that concurrent duplicates and post-crash retries observe one another, completed exactly once **under the same session fence the request was reserved on** (a completion released after the session was re-claimed writes **nothing at all** — no projection, no record change, no grant disclosed — so the request stays outstanding and the agent's next owner may re-issue it under the same id rather than inheriting a terminal failure), and answering a retry only through a session that currently owns the agent — a delayed retry of a superseded claim or an older grant can never become a new claim or the current lease, while a request orphaned by a gateway process death is **adopted into the agent's next epoch by its current session owner**, since the request's fingerprint excludes the epoch and losing it would spend the agent's only renewal nonce; `current` by **nothing, deliberately** — it is a read, writes no row, bears no idempotency and is re-evaluated on replay, because a stored reply would hand back a projection that may since have been renewed and its `NONE` answer was never ledgerable in the first place; and, for every one of them, an `agent_request_tombstone` written by the retention job, so a replay past the horizon is refused rather than inserted as new; **every operation that writes agent-bound state carries the `AgentSessionRef{agentId, fencingEpoch}` that `claim` returned — telemetry ingest and agent-bound audit included — and never a tenant**, so the contract is self-sufficient for a remote adapter; replies are explicit about commit; volume paths are batch-shaped; no port carries a secret the gateway must interpret; the command feed is a **durable poll loop** whose poll is itself a fenced reservation over `dp-core`'s deliverable index — joining ownership and the lease row, stamping what it returns — with an after-commit wake-up as a latency optimisation only — never a bare callback, never the sole path. **Seven of the nine — the implementation-ready ones** — ship an abstract contract-test kit in `dp-api`'s test-jar (the `BE-D10` publisher-kit pattern) that the in-process adapter passes in v1 and the remote adapter must pass at extraction; **`ActualStatePort` and `AgentAuditPort` may define provisional shapes but no complete durability kit and no conformance claim until design §13 AGW-20 and AGW-21 close (S5/F4)**. `dp-api` declares `AgwPortsVersion`; remotely `[N−1, N]` is supported. The agent path enters the apply engine as an `AgentDispatchAdapter` implementing `BE-D12`'s `RuntimeAdapter`: `prepare`/`materializeConfig`/`startOrUpdate`/`stop` (and a known-target rollback) insert a command and return **`IN_FLIGHT`**; `status` is a **DP-local read** of the canonical current per-replica report projection required by design §13 `AGW-20` plus heartbeat facts, never a command; an attempt whose step has a live command row resumes as **awaiting completion**, not `APPLY_INDETERMINATE` — all flagged to the `BE-D*` owners as extensions (design §3.5, §11).

⚠️ **The scope of the durable-arbiter and `COMMITTED` guarantees, stated once (S4/F3).** The nine ports fall into **four categories, and an earlier count collapsed two of them (S6/F5)**:

- **Six mutation-bearing ports with durable arbiters and `COMMITTED` semantics** — `EnrollmentPort` (the token + CSR key), `SessionRegistryPort` (`agent_claim`), `LeasePort` (`agent_lease_grant` and its projection), `CommandFeedPort` (the command row and its `offered_epoch` stamp — `pollDeliverable` **is** a write: design §3.2 has it move `QUEUED → OFFERED` or re-stamp `offered_epoch` and return "only the rows it stamped"), `CommandAckPort` (the `DELIVERED` and terminal transitions), and `TelemetryIngestPort` (the `(producer, stream_id, batch_seq)` watermark). The durable-arbiter and `COMMITTED` guarantees are **these six**.
- **One read-only port, deliberately without an arbiter** — `AgentIdentityPort`. `resolve` is **re-evaluated on every call** (design §3.1 rule 2) and ledgers nothing, so it has no `COMMITTED` semantics to promise. ⚠️ **Caching a `resolve` result under a `requestId` is FORBIDDEN, and that prohibition is the guarantee here:** a retry after a revocation **must** observe the revocation, so returning a stored earlier answer would convert a revoked identity into a live one. Listing this port among the "durable arbiter" ports — as an earlier revision did — inverted its contract.
- **Two gated ports** — `ActualStatePort` (design §13 **AGW-20**) and `AgentAuditPort` (design §13 **AGW-21**), neither of which names a durable arbiter, and **the audit port is not among the durable six** despite an earlier count saying so while the same paragraph admitted its record is gated (S5/F4).

So: **seven implementation-ready ports = six durable + one read-only**, plus **two gated**. "Seven" is a statement about *implementation readiness and kit coverage*, never about durability. They do **not** yet hold for **`ActualStatePort.report`**, which names no durable arbiter because no report row, full-input fingerprint, per-`(agent, binding, replica)` freshness watermark or canonical current projection exists in this design set; design §3.5 assigns those facts to `dp-core`'s store and this family does not specify it. **What AGW-20 must deliver before those clauses are more than provisional:** a durable **per-item** request identity — the pair **`(agent_id, item_id)`**, the agent identity **DP-resolved** from the `AgentSessionRef` and never from the wire (`I5`), from which the per-item `requestId` derives (UUIDv5 over `agent_id` + `item_id` + operation); `item_id` alone collides across agents (S7/F1); the enclosing batch message's `message_id` is transport correlation only and never an idempotency key (S6/F1) — plus the **canonical fingerprint** design §3.2 fixes verbatim (`sequence`, `binding_id`, `replica_ref`, `incarnation`, `persisted_generation`, `restart_observed_at`, `effective_hash`, `version_observation{mechanism, value, confidence}`, and the DP-resolved agent identity from the lease; excluding `item_id`, the batch `message_id`, the session id and fencing epoch, and the protocol and agent versions, each for the reason stated there); original-result replay for an identical retry and an explicit refusal for the same identity with a different payload, **including a changed `sequence` alone** (S6/F2); **the precedence between fencing and replay — authorization checks precede arbiter lookup and result disclosure**, so a stale or non-owning session is refused `STALE_EPOCH` and learns nothing about a stored result, a current-owner retry replays the stored trust and disposition without recomputing them, a revocation between attempts is a session-level refusal rather than a replay, and a binding removed between attempts leaves an already-applied report's stored result intact (S7/F2); persistence of `trust` **and** the orthogonal `disposition`; per-replica freshness; one canonical current projection that `RuntimeAdapter.status` and convergence read; either an atomic relationship with registry admission **or** an explicit durable repair protocol when the two diverge; the reporter-handoff update rule; lost-reply retry behaviour; and a transaction boundary that is visible to both the in-process and the remote adapter. Until then this port's `Committed` result, its §3.3 kit and its §9 extraction conformance are **PROVISIONAL** and are named as such in design §3.1. ⚠️ **`AgentAuditPort` carries its own gate (S4/F4, design §13 AGW-21)**: its clause here promises a durable record, and the store design §3.2 pointed at cannot hold agent-security events.

**Rationale.** "In-memory callbacks are never the sole contract" is the brief's own line, and the reason is extraction: a callback has no request id, no idempotency, no version and no test a remote adapter could pass — and, as the first review found, an after-commit hook fires only in the process that committed, which is not necessarily the process that owns the agent. Ports with the same shape in-process and remote, and a poll that is correct without the hook, are the mechanism that turns extraction into an adapter swap (`AGW-D14`). The `console-gateway` → `*-api` dependency already exists in the reactor, so the shape is not new. Mapping `status` to a command would have needed an intent row that does not exist and would have written one row per health poll.

**Rejected.** *Direct repository access from the gateway* — the coupling the brief forbids. *Spring events / `ApplicationEventPublisher` as the contract* — in-memory, unversioned, untestable remotely, process-local. *A separate `agw-api` module* — the ports are the DP's public interface to a DP-side consumer; `dp-api` already plays that role for `console-gateway`. *Every adapter operation as a wire command* — `status` cannot be, and `prepare` needs its own verb.

## `AGW-D7` — The lease audience names the logical Data Plane, and the revocation epoch is agent-scoped and fleet-scoped

`LeaseGrant.audience` (the `SM-D28` `audience` claim) is the **stable identity of the Data Plane deployment**, not the id of the process that happened to hold the agent's stream. The property `SM-D28` wants — "not replayable at another DP or at the CP" — is preserved: a lease for DP *X* is refused by DP *Y* and by the CP. What changes is that a lease is valid at *every gateway replica* of DP *X*, so failover (design §5.3) needs a new claim, not a new lease or a re-enrolment. Per-replica exclusivity is `AGW-D3`'s fencing epoch. Second, the grant carries **two** revocation epochs — `agent_revocation_epoch` (per key thumbprint) and `fleet_revocation_epoch` (per tenant, for deliberate mass revocation) — adopting the subject-scoped split `go-agent/design.md` §3.2 already requires of the agent and giving it plane-side storage (`agent_fleet_epoch`, design §5.2); the agent must never conflate them.

**This amends `SM-D28`** (`PROPOSED`) in two claims and ratifies after it, never in the same round (open item 34). The other seven claims, the one-hour duration, the 50 % renewal, the single-use nonce and the `CLOCK_SUSPECT` rule are untouched — and "untouched" is now enforced rather than asserted: the nonce is the **only** path to a lease after the first, because `LeasePort.issue` is available only while no `agent_lease` row has ever existed and an expired lease is renewed or re-enrolled (`AGW-D15`, design §4.6).

**Rationale.** Run `SM-D28` as written through design §5.3: replica `r1` dies, the agent lands on `r2`, and `r2` must refuse the lease because `audience = r1`. The agent then cannot renew (renewal needs a valid session) and cannot apply until re-enrolled by an operator with a new token. Every replica restart becomes an operator event across the fleet — the opposite of HA. `SM-D28` was written when "the DP" was one process; this decision updates the referent to the thing that is actually stable. The epoch split is not this document's invention — the agent design demands it — but a claim the agent must honour needs a row the plane can raise, and `SM-D28` names only one.

**Rejected.** *Re-mint a lease on every claim* — the CP mints (`SM-D28` role table), making every failover a CP round-trip and a CP-availability dependency for DP-local continuity. *Encode the replica set in the audience* — changes on every scale event, so every scale event invalidates every lease. *One epoch, raised fleet-wide on any compromise* — turns one compromised host into a fleet-wide outage (`go-agent/design.md` §3.2).

## `AGW-D8` — Backpressure is credits; the gateway drops nothing; credits always recover

Telemetry: the gateway grants `initial_credits` on `SessionAccept` **only from budget the ledger reserves at that moment — the grant may be zero**, so a reconnect storm admitted under `max_sessions` can never oversubscribe the budget by one batch per session; **every session-close path returns the session's unspent credits at once and its in-flight bytes when their port calls complete**, so churn cannot strand budget; each `TelemetryBatch` spends one; credits are returned on `TelemetryAck` **only after `TelemetryIngestPort` returns `Committed`**. A per-replica `telemetry_budget_bytes` bounds Σ sessions × credits × `max_batch_bytes` as an invariant of every grant, and outstanding telemetry bytes per connection are capped at half the HTTP/2 connection window; the credit ledger computes grants from remaining budget, so overload shrinks credits rather than growing memory. Because a session at zero credits could otherwise never speak again, the ledger also issues **unsolicited `TelemetryCredit`** grants as budget frees, round-robin over starved sessions. An agent with no credits buffers on disk (`AG-D9`). Command payloads are held only between `pull` and the stream write — the durable queue is `agent_command`, and notices carry no bytes. Reports and heartbeats ride HTTP/2 flow control, one outstanding port batch per session. Admission of *new sessions* is a token bucket per replica applied **before identity resolution and claim**; `Enroll`, the only certificate-less RPC, has its own bucket per source and a per-token attempt cap; overflow gets `ADMISSION_LIMITED` with `retry_after` after the handshake and **before any port call**. Planned restarts close the readiness socket, send `Drain{reconnect_after}` staggered across the drain window, wait for in-flight acks, then exit. **Drop policy, stated once:** the gateway never drops a command, never drops a telemetry batch it granted a credit for, never drops a report; the only drop in the system is the agent's counted `AG-D9` drop-oldest, reported as `gap_markers` and stored as gap rows.

**Enforcing artifacts:** the `CreditLedger` (refuses a batch with no credit: `OUT_OF_CREDITS`; issues unsolicited grants), gRPC max message size, the two admission token buckets, `CreditBudgetHoldsIT`, `ZeroCreditRecoveryIT`, `StormIsRateLimitedIT`, `EnrollStormIsLimitedIT`, `DrainCompletesInFlightIT`, `DbOutageAcksNothingIT`.

**Rationale.** Every alternative buffer is a place where "durable" becomes a lie: an in-memory telemetry queue acked on receipt loses data on gateway death and makes the freshness marker false; a gateway that drops under pressure hides gaps the dashboards promise to show (`architecture.md` §10 "no zero-fill ever"). Credits push the decision to the one component that already has a designed, counted, disk-backed buffer — the agent — and make memory on the plane a *configured* bound rather than an emergent one. Rejecting *before* the claim and before `resolve` keeps a storm from becoming a database read or write storm; limiting `Enroll` closes the one unauthenticated surface.

**Rejected.** *TCP flow control alone* — bounds bytes in the kernel, not batches in the heap, and gives no signal to shrink under DP slowness. *Ack on receipt with async write* — see above. *Server-side sampling/dropping under load* — changes telemetry meaning in the gateway, which `AGW-D2` forbids. *Credits returned only on acks* — provably deadlocks a starved session (first review, F10). *An unconditional `initial_credits = 1`* — the Codex review priced it: 2 000 reconnecting sessions at 64 KiB reserve 128 MiB the ledger never had.

## `AGW-D9` — One ingest port for three sources, keyed by a DP-resolved producer; the v1 agent telemetry transport is the gRPC session, with the broker transport recorded as a gated option

`TelemetryIngestPort.ingest(IngestBatch)` is the single Data Plane write path for MI telemetry, called by the gateway (agent samples, forwarded unchanged), by `dp-core`'s Cache poller and by `mesh-bridge`'s broker-derived event consumer (design §6.1). The batch names its **producer** — the `AgentSessionRef{agentId, fencingEpoch}` or `DeclaredInstance{instanceId}` — and **carries no tenant**: `dp-core` resolves the producer it owns, fences the epoch for agent sources, refuses a batch whose declared target the identity does not own (`TENANT_MISMATCH`), and **applies producer-specific source identity at the port boundary**. For an `AgentSessionRef` batch the label keys are exactly `binding_id`, `replica_id`, `incarnation_id`, all uuids, the binding one the lease names and the other two ids **the DP itself minted** in the `AGW-D16` registry for that binding — everything else rejected with a counted metric (the `architecture.md` §8 cardinality-cap shape; the artifact behind "no agent-chosen string is a telemetry label" — the Solution Package schema declares no metric labels today, so there is no package-declared set to validate against and this decision does not pretend there is; MI-declared labels are routed as design §13 AGW-16). For a `DeclaredInstance` batch the keys are `binding_id`, the declared inventory's `placement_id` and a `source_key` **the DP observed itself** — the endpoint it polled or the subscription it consumed — because those paths have no agent and no trusted report to mint a registry id from, and on them the DP *is* the collector, so the string is its own observation rather than a producer assertion; those producers **use no registry at all**. Both shapes normalize to one series identity inside `dp-core`, whose `source_key` surrogate is a registry `replica_id` on the agent path and a `placement_id` on the declared paths. `dp-core` resolves series in batch, writes one multi-row insert per batch, records `(tenant_id, producer, stream_id, batch_seq)` in `telemetry_ingest_watermark` in the same transaction, and replies `Committed{highWatermarkSeq}` after *that* transaction commits, coalesced or not. A replayed batch at or below the watermark is `Committed` without insert. No per-sample synchronous write exists between collector and insert. Gaps are rows. **In v1 the agent's samples travel on the `Telemetry` stream of its mTLS session (option T1).** Publishing agent samples to the customer's broker and consuming them via `mesh-bridge` (option T2) is **recorded, not chosen**, as a future transport adapter over the same port, gated on: `BE-D5` ratified; open items 32/33 closed (per-agent broker identity is the same ACL problem); open item 35 resolved (the vendor Go API is cgo; `AG-D1` is cgo-free); broker reachability from every agent host established for the install.

**Rationale.** T1 and T2 were run through the same scenarios (design session, 2026-09-08): backpressure, gateway death, broker outage, tenant binding, enrolment, network posture, spool cost, agent binary. T2's real advantage — the broker's spool absorbs bursts and the consumer paces itself — is matched on T1 by credits plus the agent's disk buffer, at the cost of the gateway holding a bounded budget. T2's costs are structural today: a second outbound channel and a second credential per agent (enrolment must provision broker identity — the `BE-D5` mechanism, measured single-tenant, blocked by items 32/33), broker reachability from exactly the segmented hosts that force `host-local` in the first place (`architecture.md` §10 forcing functions), guaranteed-messaging spool on a broker SM does not own, and a cgo dependency in the agent. T1 adds **no new gate dependency** to the agent path. The port is transport-agnostic so that T2 can replace T1 later without changing ingest semantics — which is exactly the "future scale-out must not change telemetry meaning" requirement. The producer reference, rather than a tenant field, is what makes the `I5` claim enforceable at the type level: the caller has nowhere to put a tenant.

**Rejected.** *T2 as v1* — inherits the `BE-D5` blockers and the cgo collision into the agent's critical path. *Two ingest paths* (agent → its own table) — two meanings of "sample" and a second `I5` surface. *Ack on receipt* — see `AGW-D8`. *A `tenantId` on the batch "stamped by the gateway"* — the first review showed it made `TenantFromIdentityOnlyTest` unpassable by construction. *One label shape for all three producers* — round 6 showed the three-uuid rule demanded registry ids that only a trusted **agent** report can mint (`agent_replica.registered_by_agent` is `NOT NULL`), so the Cache poller and the broker consumer could not have satisfied the port they are documented as calling; the fix is producer-specific identity, not a nullable registry column, which would have let a non-agent write the agent's own trust anchor.

## `AGW-D10` — HA routing is poll-based: any replica accepts, the claim decides, the gateway exposes nothing to `dp-core`; v1 runs one replica

Agents connect to one DNS name (design §5.1) — in front of an **L4 TCP-passthrough** balancer once there is more than one replica — that terminates nothing, needs no stickiness, honours draining, keeps idle timeout above 3 × keepalive, and health-checks a **mute readiness socket** the gateway opens only after a successful `SessionRegistryPort` probe. Any replica accepts any agent; ownership follows `AGW-D3`'s claim. Command routing to the owning replica is a **durable poll** the gateway runs — `CommandFeedPort.pollDeliverable(replicaId, ownedAgentsWithEpochs)` every `command_poll_interval` over `dp-core`'s deliverable index — with an after-commit wake-up as a latency optimisation that may be absent (another process committed) or lost without any command being lost; a command committed anywhere is delivered within one poll interval (design §8 row 18). The gateway's only inbound network surface is the agent port. **v1 ships one gateway replica** because the Data Plane deployable is one process (design §2.3); the multi-replica machinery is exercised by the integration harness with two gateway instances against one `dp-core`, and deployed at extraction (`AGW-D14`) or after a multi-instance DP decision (design §13 AGW-14).

**Rationale.** Layer 7 balancing would re-terminate mTLS and forward identity in a header — `I5`'s definition of client-supplied tenant input. Stickiness is not needed because the connection is inherently pinned and ownership is a row. A poll the gateway initiates removes the need for a `dp-core`→gateway RPC endpoint, a replica address registry the DP must trust, and a second mTLS trust relationship — and, unlike an after-commit hook, it is correct when the committing transaction ran in a different process, which the first review showed is the normal case once ownership is cluster-wide. The readiness gate turns "database down" into "no new connections" instead of "connect, fail to claim, retry" — the self-inflicted storm. Saying "one replica in v1" out loud is what keeps the HA section from being fake.

**Rejected.** *Push from `dp-core` to the owning replica* — an inbound endpoint on the gateway, a replica directory, a trust relationship, and a partition mode where `dp-core` pushes to a replica that no longer owns the agent. *After-commit hook as the delivery mechanism* — process-local; silently strands commands committed elsewhere. *A per-agent consistent-hash to replicas* — the balancer cannot see the agent id under passthrough, and a hash is a routing hint, not an ownership record.

## `AGW-D11` — Compatibility window `[N−1, N]`; closed versioned capability enum; append-only wire evolution

`SessionOpen.protocol_version` is an integer; the gateway accepts `N−1` and `N`, rejecting older with `SessionReject(PROTOCOL_VERSION_UNSUPPORTED, min_agent_version)`. `EnrollRequest.protocol_versions_supported[]` fails enrolment early. Capabilities are a closed enum (`apply-config`, `lifecycle`, `report-only`, plus what `AG-D*` adds), versioned with the protocol; `required_capabilities[]` comes from the target's package (`B/D10`); a shortfall is `CAPABILITY_MISMATCH` and Governance marks the target "agent upgrade required". `min_agent_version` per target comes from the package via `dp-core`; the gateway compares and rejects with `UPGRADE_REQUIRED`. The port contract carries `AgwPortsVersion` with the same window for gateway↔`dp-core` after extraction (in v1's single JVM it is a compile-time fact). Evolution rules for the eventual `.proto`: fields only added, never renumbered or retyped; enums only appended; removed behaviour becomes a rejected code.

**Rationale.** `B/D10` already locks capability advertisement and min-agent-version; this decision gives them a wire home and a window wide enough for a rolling upgrade and narrow enough that the gateway never carries three protocol dialects. Stating the evolution rules before the `.proto` exists means the `.proto` author inherits them rather than discovering them at the first breaking change.

**Rejected.** *Free-string capabilities* — the fork audit's M6; unbounded vocabulary means unbounded authorization surface. *Supporting all versions forever* — every code path becomes conditional and untested combinations multiply.

## `AGW-D12` — Capacity is a model with named inputs and two measured constants; metrics and limits are part of the design

Inputs `A` (agents, one per Target), `W` (workloads — the Target's replicas — per agent), `S, P, P′, B, R, D`; measured `C` (sessions per replica at target memory) and `I` (ingest batches/s one `dp-core` commits at target latency); formulas for sample rate, batch rate, replica count with N−1 headroom **(an extraction-time formula; v1 is one replica)**, per-replica memory, storm ramp and daily storage (design §5.9). The named gateway and `dp-core` metrics (§5.7) and the configuration limits surfaced as gauges (§5.8) are normative parts of the design, so a reviewer can compare a running install to the model. Numbers such as "50 nodes × 20 pods" are **inputs to the model, never conclusions**, and "target" in the model means exactly what `B/D7` and `agent_identity.target_id` mean — one per agent.

**Rationale.** `L12` — never report a derived count as a verified figure. A capacity *number* written today would be a recalled fact; a *model* with measured constants is a verifiable one, and it names which knob (`B`, `admission_rate`, `N_g`) moves the binding constraint for a given install. With `N_dp = 1` the model makes `I` visible as the ceiling of the whole path, which is the honest statement of v1. The storage formula feeds `SM-D8`'s sizing model (open item 2); it does not replace it.

**Rejected.** *A headline agents-per-replica figure* — unmeasured, and it would be quoted. *Overloading "target" for pods* — the first review caught the capacity model redefining the project's most load-bearing noun three sections after the identity table pinned it.

## `AGW-D13` — Agent authorization is identity + lease + one target, never a second human RBAC; `I5`/`I6`/least privilege by construction

An agent's authorization is *which target, which capabilities, until when* — the mTLS certificate mapped by `AgentIdentityPort` to one `target_id`, plus the `SM-D28` lease's `capabilities[]` and `expires_at`. No human verbs, roles or policy language exist on the agent path; operator actions *about* agents are `D10` verbs routed to the amendment paper (design §13 AGW-10). **`I5`:** tenant comes only from identity resolution, re-derived by `dp-core` from `agentId` inside every agent-bound port; the wire has no tenant field and `IngestBatch` has no tenant field; a batch naming a target the identity does not own is `TENANT_MISMATCH`; the `SessionOpen.resume` cursor is a hint that advances nothing; every new table keys and FKs on `tenant_id`. **Capability enforcement** is `dp-core`'s before it is the agent's: a command whose `required_capability` the lease lacks is refused as `CAPABILITY_MISSING` before any notice (`AGW-D4`), so a `report-only` agent is never offered a lifecycle verb. **`I6`:** rendered configuration travels only inside the mTLS session as opaque `CommandOffer.payload_bytes`, held by the gateway only between `pull` and the stream write, never logged (redacting log value), never persisted (no datasource), never in a metric or event, never in a blob store — a payload that does not fit one message is refused at render time; `config_sha256` over sent bytes is an integrity check distinct from the `I6` hash-over-references of record; tokens and nonces are logged as hashes; telemetry labels are producer-specific and never a producer-asserted string — an agent batch carries the lease's binding plus two ids the DP minted in the `AGW-D16` registry, a `DeclaredInstance` batch carries inventory identifiers plus a DP-observed `source_key` — and everything else is rejected and counted (`AGW-D9`), so **no agent-chosen string reaches `metrics_raw`**; the agent's replica strings reach only the registry, grammar-checked per label, capped per binding and per replica, once each, audited — and because a secret that is itself a valid lowercase hostname could pass that grammar once, **this `I6` clause is not ratifiable until design §13 AGW-17 is closed or the residual is explicitly accepted, and `AGW-D16` as a whole additionally requires design §13 AGW-20 — its retirement clauses also §13 AGW-19** (design §12 limit 14). Where `I6` says "never persisted", this design says exactly what could still persist and gates on it rather than claiming it away. **Least privilege:** the gateway module holds no database and no broker credential, injects none and can look none up (`AGW-D2`); in v1 the shared process does hold the DP datasource, and process-level isolation is an extraction property; the DP runtime role's grants on the eleven new tables are column-scoped and `DELETE`-free (six in design §7.4, five in the sketch's §7, whose sequence also needs an explicit `USAGE` grant) — **both grant sets are executable `GRANT` statements since S9/C2, not prose** —, the claim ledger is `SELECT`/`INSERT`-only, the tombstone table is `SELECT`-only, — ⚠️ **and that, with the identity columns and the sequence `USAGE` grant, is the WHOLE privilege boundary: measured on PG 14.24 (S10/F3), the runtime role can also execute the registry's retirement sweeps, whose columns it must already be able to write for `B4`, `B2` and step 3c, so the migrator is only the recommended scheduler and central open item 39 leaves scheduling and ownership unsettled** — and the lease ledger's `UPDATE` is confined by column scope and by the `terminal_once` trigger — **a `plpgsql` function and a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger in the sketch's §2.1 DDL since S9/C4, where it had been pseudo-code** — to **exactly three** transitions — completion, a post-deadline takeover that increments the attempt and never lowers the epoch, and an epoch transfer that raises it — so a `PENDING → PENDING` fingerprint rewrite and every write to a terminal row are refused while the two recoveries `SM-D28`'s single-use nonce requires stay expressible ([`lease-and-registry-sketch.md`](lease-and-registry-sketch.md) §2.1, §7; the six tables' own grants are design §7.4). **Revocation** is epoch-based (agent or fleet), refused **inside the claim statement**, and checked on renewal and every heartbeat reply, so it lands within one heartbeat interval and a revoked agent cannot reconnect. **A nonce-free lease is granted at most once per identity** — `issue` requires that no `agent_lease` row has ever existed, projection rows are never deleted on expiry, and an agent whose lease expired renews under its single-use nonce or is refused `RE_ENROLL_REQUIRED`, so neither an expiry nor a retention sweep re-opens the no-nonce path for an identity that has ever **held** a lease (design §4.6). ⚠️ **One identity is not covered and is stated rather than claimed away:** where the only `issue` ended `FAILED`, no projection row was ever written and the ledger's index row is pruned with the ledger, so a new request id past `ledger_retention` is admitted — design §12 limit 19a, and (L7) measures it. No nonce is bypassed there (none was ever issued); what can result is two live CP grants for one identity. **Capability and scope authority** is the durable `agent_lease` row (`AGW-D15`) `dp-core` projects from every issued or renewed grant, joined by the delivery poll in one statement together with the ownership row, so a `dp-core` restart re-derives nothing, a stale or non-owning replica is offered nothing, a command for a target or binding the current lease does not name is refused before any notice, and **an actual-state report for a binding the lease does not name is classified `UNTRUSTED` and satisfies no convergence gate; durable persistence is the open design §13 `AGW-20` contract** however active the lease. The row's admission inputs are typed and CHECK-validated — non-negative ceilings and margin, and `lease_duration > max(ceiling) + margin` — so a malformed grant is refused (`GRANT_INVALID`) rather than authorizing work after expiry. **The lease is bound to the certificate**: `LeaseGrant.cnf_thumbprint` must equal the identity's certificate thumbprint, the renewal upsert refuses any other (`LEASE_KEY_MISMATCH`), and the poll joins on it, so a grant lifted from one agent grants nothing elsewhere. **Enrolment replay** returns the stored `Enrolled` to the same token *and* the same CSR and `TOKEN_CONSUMED` to anyone else — and because a replayed CSR proves nothing about key possession, that reply is **public material only** (certificate, trust bundle, agent id). **No report credential is issued in this draft** — not at enrolment (replay would leak it) and not at first accept (no idempotent issuance arbiter is specified); it stays routed (design §13 AGW-12, §12 limit 16). **Trust** on reports is decided by `dp-core` from its own lease record **at receive time** and the call's epoch — never from the agent's `sent_at`, which is display-only. **Replay** is refused by TLS 1.3, single-use nonces, request-id idempotency, epochs on every agent-bound write and watermarks. **Every refusal** is a closed code with no hint (`B/D8`) and is audited through `AgentAuditPort`, per event not per message, sampled under the limiters — ⚠️ **and "audited" is behaviour required of a store that does not exist yet**: the record this pointed at cannot hold an agent-security event, so the durable half is design §13 **AGW-21** and, until it closes, this clause is not implementation-ready (S4/F4). **Enrolment**, the one certificate-less surface, is admission-limited per source and capped per token.

**Rationale.** The brief's warning — "do not invent a second human authorization system" — is easy to violate by accident: an "agent admin" role, an "agent may read fleet" permission. Keeping the agent's authorization to three facts the lease already carries means G2 (open item 24) gates the *screens about agents*, not the agent path itself, and the console's deny-by-default catalog stays the only human catalog. The rest of this decision is the `I5`/`I6` audit the checklist demands, written as artifacts rather than adjectives; the first review's four security findings (trust from an agent timestamp, an unlimited `Enroll`, a `blob_ref` store, an unfenced telemetry port) are each closed by a named artifact above.

**Rejected.** *Agent-asserted trust or tenant fields* — laundering. *Trust from `sent_at`* — laundering by backdating. *A gateway DB role "for audit"* — every audit row goes through a port, so the role has no purpose except to become a hole. *A `blob_ref` fetch* — a second unauthenticated-adjacent surface and a place where resolved secrets rest.

## `AGW-D14` — Extraction is adapters only; what may not change is enumerated

At extraction, `agent-gateway` becomes its own deployable and the port adapters change from in-process calls to mTLS RPC against `dp-core` (a reviewed `contracts/` artifact, design §13 AGW-11); the command feed becomes an RPC poll with the wake-up possibly absent; the gateway principal authenticates to `dp-core` and still holds no DB role; the replica count becomes `N_g` per the capacity model. **May not change:** port semantics (request ids, epochs, replies), apply semantics, persistence ownership, telemetry meaning and tenant derivation, and the agent-visible protocol. **Acceptance:** the remote adapters pass every `AGW-D6` contract kit that exists unchanged — **the seven implementation-ready ports; `ActualStatePort` and `AgentAuditPort` have no complete durability kit to pass until design §13 AGW-20 and AGW-21 close (S5/F4)**; `FailoverRedeliversOnceIT`, `SplitBrainFencedIT` and `NonOwningCommitDeliveredIT` pass with two gateway processes; `C` and `I` are re-measured. No design document is re-opened.

**Rationale.** This is the brief's goal stated as a test. If extraction needs a new test, a semantic changed; if it needs a design change, the boundary was wrong. Enumerating the frozen list now is what lets a reviewer at extraction time say "you changed telemetry meaning" instead of arguing about intent. Because v1 runs one replica, extraction is also the first moment the HA machinery runs in production — which is why its acceptance tests are the same tests the v1 harness runs with two in-process instances.

**Rejected.** *"Extraction is a packaging change"* — `BE-D3`'s R5 review withdrew that exact phrase for the browser↔mesh path; this family does not repeat it. Extraction is an adapter change with named acceptance tests.

## `AGW-D15` — The DP-side lease store: three operations, a recorded and fenced grant path, and a monotonic projection the delivery gate reads

**This decision states properties. Its mechanism — DDL, the three-step SQL, the DCL and the execution obligations — is [`lease-and-registry-sketch.md`](lease-and-registry-sketch.md), a separately gated document that is *not* part of the `AGW-D1`…`AGW-D14` ratification set.** What follows is what must hold; what the sketch carries is one way to make it hold — **executed on PG 14 and measured, not ratified and not implemented** (S11/F5): [`evidence/pg14-execution.md`](evidence/pg14-execution.md), `8551e5c`, `PostgreSQL 14.24 (Debian 14.24-1.pgdg13+2)`, 224 legs, 162 `OK` / 60 `REFUSED` / 2 `N/A-engine` / 0 `FAIL`. This decision's own gate — design §13 AGW-18 — is untouched by that run and remains open, so `AGW-D15` stays `PROPOSED` and unratifiable.

**The three operations are deliberately separate, because "return the lease that exists", "grant the first lease" and "grant the next one" are three different authorizations.** `LeasePort.current` returns an unexpired, certificate-bound projection with no CP call — and is a **read**: it writes nothing, bears no idempotency and is re-evaluated on replay, because a stored reply would hand back a snapshot the next renewal may already have replaced, and its `NONE` answer was never a recordable outcome at all. `LeasePort.issue` takes the single nonce-free grant of an identity's life, on the mTLS-proven identity and the CP's enrolment approval, and is permitted **only while no `agent_lease` row has ever existed** for the agent — projection rows are never deleted on expiry, so their existence is the durable "granted before" test, and the durable **arbiter** is an index admitting one `issue` request row per identity, so a second is `ISSUE_NOT_ALLOWED` whatever a caller believes. `LeasePort.renew` takes every later grant, presenting the consumed nonce. An agent whose lease has expired therefore **renews**, and if the CP refuses its nonce the session is refused `RE_ENROLL_REQUIRED` — an operator event. `SM-D28`'s single-use nonce semantics are preserved exactly; `AGW-D7` amends the `audience` and epoch claims and nothing else.

**Properties every `issue`/`renew` request must have.**

1. **Recorded before the external call.** The request exists durably *before* the CP is called, so concurrent duplicates and post-crash retries observe one another rather than both calling the CP: a duplicate sees `IN_PROGRESS`, a changed fingerprint is `REQUEST_ID_REUSED`, a request whose record retention removed is `REQUEST_EXPIRED` from a tombstone (never a second grant), a second `issue` is `ISSUE_NOT_ALLOWED` — refused by the projection row's existence, which nothing deletes, **and** by a ledger index that additionally refuses two concurrent first-issues under different request ids but is pruned with the ledger row (the uncovered case: a first `issue` that ended `FAILED`, design §12 limit 19a).
2. **Answered only to a live owner, and "owner" is a tuple.** A retry receives **its own** request's outcome and only through a session that **currently owns the agent**; a caller at any other epoch learns nothing — not the state, not the grant, not that the request exists. **A request id authorizes nothing by itself:** every statement that completes a request is bound to the caller's `AgentSessionRef` — the agent **and** the epoch it carries — the gateway replica that owns the session, and the replica that owns the attempt. One gateway replica commonly owns sessions for many agents, so a completion selected by request id and fenced only on "some session this replica owns" would let one agent's session complete another agent's request; the tenant is still derived from the durable row, never from caller input (`I5`).
3. **Completed exactly once, under the fence — and a lost fence writes nothing.** Completion joins the *record's own* agent and epoch. A completion released after the session was re-claimed therefore writes **no projection and no record change**: the request stays outstanding and the agent's next owner may re-issue it under the same id, while the superseded caller is told nothing and its grant is discarded. Round 9 found the alternative — completing such a request as a terminal failure — self-defeating: a terminal record is one no later owner can re-drive, so the CP contract of AGW-18 could never be consulted for the very case it exists for. A second *write* is impossible by construction, not by convention; a second *request* is never needed. **Ownership of the attempt is one locked decision taken before anything is projected** — round 10 found the projection running as a data-modifying CTE beside an unlocked ownership read, so a superseded attempt could install its older grant into the projection while its ledger write matched nothing.
4. **Never abandoned — two recoveries for two failures.** A **gateway process death** raises the agent's epoch, and the request survives it by an **authenticated epoch transfer**: the request's identity is epoch-independent, its epoch binding is not, and only the agent's current session owner may adopt it. A **stalled or dead CP call** is recovered by a **bounded takeover under the same request id**, one owner at a time, capped, ending in `FAILED('TIMEOUT')` and `RE_ENROLL_REQUIRED` rather than a silent nonce-free grant. Both exist because on a `renew` the nonce is single-use: abandoning a request for a new id would spend the agent's only renewal credential on a grant no record holds. **Both are fenced in their own statements** on the record's agent and the caller's *still-current* ownership, so neither can bind a request to an epoch that has already moved. **What remains outside the DP** is narrow and named: whether the CP returns the original grant when the same request id is presented again. With AGW-18 honoured it does and the nonce is spent exactly once; without it the CP refuses a consumed nonce, the record completes `FAILED('CP_REFUSED')` and the session is refused `RE_ENROLL_REQUIRED` — an operator event on a bounded path (design §12 limit 17, §13 AGW-18). Nothing in the DP's statements forecloses the repair, which is the property round 9 had to force. **Adoption transfers the whole ownership tuple** — the epoch (strictly up), the attempt owner and the attempt deadline, with the attempt *number* unchanged, because a gateway death is not a timeout and must not spend the takeover budget that protects the single-use nonce; re-arming the deadline is also what stops the migrator's orphan sweep terminalizing a recovery it has just handed to a live owner. **And every caller-driven terminal write is fenced like any other agent-bound write** — round 10 found the exhausted-request completion exempt, which let any session that learned another agent's request id terminalize that agent's renewal; a request **nobody** drives is ended by the migrator instead, which is the one actor with no session to present.
5. **Validated before it is trusted.** A malformed or impossible grant (negative ceiling or margin, a window shorter than max ceiling + margin, an unknown capability, a foreign `cnf`) is refused in `dp-core` **before any write** and recorded `FAILED('GRANT_INVALID')`; database constraints are a backstop behind the validator, never its control flow.
6. **One current lease, totally ordered.** The projection advances only to a strictly newer grant under a total order, so ties cannot yield two "current" grants; it carries `lease_jti`, the certificate binding (`SM-D28`'s key confirmation), the one target, the bindings, the capabilities, both revocation epochs, the audience, the window, and **the four verb ceilings and the admission margin exactly as delivered**, as typed and CHECK-validated columns. The delivery poll (`AGW-D4`) and the report intake (`AGW-D13`) read **that row and nothing else** — not live configuration, not the wire, not process memory — so a `dp-core` restart re-derives nothing.
7. **Prunable.** Records are bounded by `ledger_retention` and the idempotency horizon by `tombstone_retention` (design §12 limits 18, 19), and **nothing about the projection may block that pruning** — which is why its provenance columns are not foreign keys. A CP grant issued for a request that never completed — or one **discarded after a fence loss**, where the completing caller drops the grant and writes nothing so the request stays recoverable (§3.3) — is wasted authority bounded by its TTL (design §12 limit 17). **And a request nobody ever returns for is ended by the migrator's orphan sweep**, never by an arbitrary session: `FAILED('TIMEOUT')` after `orphan_grace`, terminal, then prunable — so "prunable" is true of every record eventually, not only of the ones a caller completes.

**Rationale.** `SM-D28` names "the DP-side lease store" as remaining for the agent sub-spec and specifies nothing about it; `AGW-D4`'s gate cannot be settled (`L19`) without a record to read, and two review rounds showed that a gate described as reading "the current lease" was reading nothing. Persisting the ceilings and margin *as delivered* is what makes the DP's admission decision and the agent's ([`../../go-agent/design.md`](../../go-agent/design.md) §4.3) evaluate the same inequality with the same inputs after either side restarts or configuration changes — a snapshot on both ends, not live config on one. Giving the store its own decision keeps open item 34 honest: ratifying `AGW-D7` approves two claim changes, not a fifth table and its write semantics. Keeping its **mechanism** in its own document keeps the round-6 and round-7 meta-finding honest too: a subsystem this size, gated on a decision the family does not own, must be reviewable — and refusable — on its own.

**Rejected.** *Deriving capabilities from the identity or the package at poll time* — that is what a lease exists to bound; it would offer lifecycle commands to an agent whose lease was narrowed. *Reading ceilings from live platform configuration* — the agent holds the delivered snapshot, so a configuration change between grant and poll would make the two gates disagree. *Folding the store into `AGW-D7`* — a claims amendment and a table are different governance objects. *A single `renewal_request_id` column as the idempotency arbiter* — round 4 showed it returns a delayed older retry the *newer* grant and cannot refuse a reused id with different contents. *A record written after the CP answers* — round 5 showed it arbitrates nothing before the external side effect, and that "recorded, then refused by a constraint" is impossible in one statement. *Ceilings as a JSON blob* — a CHECK cannot validate its values. *A merged `currentOrIssue`* — round 6 measured it as a scope breach against `SM-D28`: it issued a nonce-free grant whenever no unexpired projection existed, so the single-use nonce could be bypassed by waiting. *Abandoning a timed-out request for a new id* — it can permanently consume the only renewal nonce. *Foreign keys from the projection to the record* — they blocked the retention job they were meant to protect. *Making `current` idempotency-bearing* — round 7's finding: it cannot be. Half its answers (`NONE`) are not recordable and the other half are time-sensitive, so a "stored reply" is either absent or wrong; the honest statement is that it is a read, and the flow's idempotency is the claim record's plus the grant record's. *"Once per identity" as prose* — round 7 again: without an index it was a predicate a caller could race, and two concurrent first-issues under different request ids would both have passed.

**Ordering and gate.** Depends on `SM-D28` (the claim set) and `AGW-D7`; ratifies after both — **and only with the CP issuance contract of design §13 AGW-18**, without which the timeout recovery has no correct form. It is **not** ratified as part of the `AGW-D1`…`AGW-D14` set. Until then the mechanism is designed so the ports and tests can name it (design §1), not settled.

## `AGW-D16` — The DP-minted replica registry: two levels, a per-label grammar, live caps, one active incarnation, and retirement

**This decision states properties. Its mechanism — the three tables' DDL, the registration transaction, retirement and retention, the DCL and the execution obligations — is [`lease-and-registry-sketch.md`](lease-and-registry-sketch.md), a separately gated document that is *not* part of the `AGW-D1`…`AGW-D14` ratification set.**

The registry is populated **only** by `TRUSTED` `ActualStateReport`s and read by `TelemetryIngestPort` as the authority for an agent batch's labels (design §6.1). Properties:

1. **Two levels, two lifetimes.** The **stable** level mints one id per `(tenant, binding, replica_ref)` and **reuses it on every later report for that pair** — it *is* the series `source_key` surrogate of [`../../architecture.md`](../../architecture.md) §8, part of series identity, so it must not move when the workload restarts. The **ephemeral** level mints one id per **activation** of an `incarnation`, living in `labels_jsonb` **outside** series identity, so a restart is visible without splitting the series. A row is **never re-activated**: an incarnation string a replica has carried before gets a new row and a new id, because re-activating the old row would keep its original `registered_at` and give it an acceptance window spanning its own successor's lifetime — the disjointness property 6 depends on. `ReportAck` returns both, and they are the **only** values an agent may put in `TelemetryBatch` labels.
2. **Only a trusted agent report writes here, and the first one needs no incumbent to displace.** A `(binding, replica_ref)` reported for the first time has no reporter row and no minted id, so the reporter gate is **bypassed** rather than passed, and the initial reporter is the **authenticated** reporting agent resolved from its mTLS identity — never a value the report asserts. `DeclaredInstance` producers (Cache pull, broker-derived) **use no registry at all** — their source identity is the inventory's `placement_id` plus a DP-observed `source_key` (`AGW-D9`) — which is why the stable row's reporting-agent column stays `NOT NULL`: only a trusted agent report may write the agent path's own trust anchor.
3. **A closed grammar, checked per label.** `replica_ref` must satisfy the RFC 1123 hostname grammar — lowercase, per-label ≤ 63, total ≤ 253, no empty label, no leading or trailing hyphen — and `incarnation` must match exactly one of three alternatives (uuid, 12–64-char hex, ≤ 20-digit decimal). Both are database constraints; invalid content produces disposition `REFUSED(REPLICA_REF_INVALID)` without changing the lease/epoch/scope trust verdict.
4. **Caps that are an artifact, and — at the stable level — that count the living.** Both levels are capped by configuration (`max_replicas_per_binding`, `max_incarnations_per_replica`); a registration beyond either is refused `REPLICA_CAP_EXCEEDED`, the report's **disposition** records that reason (its `trust` is design §4.8's separate verdict and a cap refusal does not change it), and the refusal is counted — so one valid agent cannot grow the registry without bound. The **stable** cardinality is a **locked row**, not a count a concurrent pair of registrations can both pass, it counts **active** replicas only, and it moves only when a slot is consumed or released — never on a re-report of a living replica, and never past the cap by un-retiring a returning name: a replica idle for `replica_idle_retirement` is retired and its slot released, because replica names churn across deployments and a cap over the historical population would make every long-lived binding permanently exhausted. The **ephemeral** cap is *not* symmetric and the decision says so: it is a count over active plus retired-but-unpruned incarnations, released by `registry_retention` rather than by retirement. **What serializes it is the binding-wide cardinality row, not the reporting identity's lock:** every registration on a binding takes that row `FOR UPDATE` and holds it to transaction end, so it arbitrates **both** levels; the identity lock orders one agent's own reports and is not a cross-agent arbiter. For the ephemeral cap that serialization is **over-determined and therefore has no fixture at all**: two transactions could only contend for one replica's ephemeral count if two agents reported that replica at once, which the per-identity lock, the reporter gate **and** the `FOR UPDATE` the transaction takes on the replica's active activation each already forbid independently — so no test can exhibit it, and the design says so instead of naming a layer where it might (three successive attempts at such a fixture were each impossible; S4/F1). The cardinality row's lock is proven where it is reachable and load-bearing: the **stable** cap, two distinct agents registering two new `replica_ref`s on one binding. **And a refused registration changes nothing, which is a property of the transaction's shape and not of its ordering:** the registration transaction reads and decides in a phase that performs no registry write, and enters its write phase only once every gate — reporter, both caps, watermark — has passed, so a refusal produces a `REFUSED(reason)` disposition without changing trust and leaves the registry unchanged; report persistence and metrics are outside this transaction and tracked by design §13 `AGW-20`. **And retirement is the migrator's, in two stages:** an incarnation is retired by a successor's registration **or**, when a replica simply *vanishes*, by a sweep of incarnations whose DP-observed liveness has gone stale — without which a stable replica would never become idle and its cap slot would never return, which is the exhaustion this property claims to prevent. Both stages run on the retention job's cadence rather than inside a report, because running them inside the registration transaction would put **both** of the sketch §7 branch tails in one transaction — registration's `agent_replica` → `agent_replica_incarnation` and retirement's `agent_replica_incarnation` → `agent_replica` — which is the one thing that model forbids (S9/F1); keeping them in separate transactions, each taking the binding's cardinality row `FOR UPDATE` first, is what makes the tails mutually exclusive rather than cyclic. **The silence stage is conditional on a periodic actual-state report existing at all** — the only thing that refreshes DP-observed liveness — which is an ask on the agent design (design §13 AGW-19), not something this family may assume. And although only the stable level has a cardinality *counter*, that row's `FOR UPDATE` **serializes every registration on the binding**, so the ephemeral cap is not raceable either: round 9's claimed cross-agent overshoot contradicted this design's own locking and is withdrawn.
5. **Exactly one active incarnation per replica, and order respected — within one agent.** The one-active rule is an index, not prose; the transaction that admits a new incarnation retires the previous one; and a report older than the active activation's own reporter-scoped watermark changes **nothing** — **and "trusted" is not what that decides**: `trust` is computed from the lease, the epoch and the scope alone (design §4.8), so such a report is `TRUSTED` and its `disposition` is `STALE`, two orthogonal answers rather than one contradictory label. It must also not move the actual-state projection, which design §8 rows 6 and 43 require per replica; where the per-`(agent, binding, replica)` watermark, canonical current projection and disposition are **stored** is `dp-core`'s report intake and is explicitly tracked as design §13 `AGW-20`, not specified here. **That comparison is per agent, because the sequence is:** a report's `sequence` is a counter the reporting agent keeps, so it orders that agent's own reports and says nothing across agents. A replica whose workload is rescheduled onto another host is reported by a *different* agent, whose counter may be smaller; such a report is ordered by DP receive order and treated as a genuine change. The row therefore records **which agent** asserted it — and the *stable* row records **which agent may report it now**, with a change of reporter admitted only as a handoff after the incumbent has gone silent for a grace period, because ordering across agents by receive order alone let a delayed report from a displaced agent retire the live incarnation and reactivate a dead one. The per-agent order itself is a **watermark on the activation row, scoped to the reporter it belongs to**, advanced only by a report above it from that same reporter, so an old delayed report cannot refresh liveness either. An admitted handoff **re-seeds** that watermark to the admitting report — **on the activation the report names, and on no other** — which is what lets a new reporter whose own counter starts low refresh liveness immediately instead of being mute until it passes the displaced agent's number; when the same report also changes the incarnation the outgoing activation is retired carrying the **displaced** reporter's watermark and last-seen values, because those columns describe the latest trusted report naming *that* activation and a report naming its successor is not one — and the immutable creation audit (`registered_by_agent`, `registered_by_report`) is never rewritten, which is why the watermark is its own pair of columns.
6. **Retirement is a timestamp; deletion is predicated.** Retired incarnations are pruned after `registry_retention`; a retired stable row only once nothing — no incarnation, no `series` row — still names its id. Because each activation is its own row, one replica's successive windows are **disjoint** by construction rather than by a tie-break. And **a retired incarnation is not a live identity**: a sample labelled with one is accepted only if its timestamp falls inside that incarnation's own registration window — **half-open, `[registered_at, retired_at)`**, so a replica's successive incarnations have disjoint windows and the retire instant belongs to the incoming one — else `INCARNATION_RETIRED` with a gap marker recorded. ⚠️ **That comparison reads the agent's own `ts`** — the one place on this path where admission uses the agent's clock — so it stops a dead incarnation from labelling *current* readings but not from carrying **backdated** ones; design §12 limit 20 states the residual and §8 row 44 measures it, and it routes with the AGW-17 gate this decision already carries.

**Rationale.** The registry was `AGW-D9`/`AGW-D13`'s mechanism until round 6 made three things visible at once: the single table's uniqueness made the stable id change on **every** incarnation, contradicting the stable/ephemeral split the architecture's series identity depends on; nothing bounded its growth; and its grammar, its caps and its unverified residual are a governance question with a different answer date than "is the ingest port one port". Round 7 then found that its caps and its one-active rule were still *prose* — a race and an exhaustion bug respectively — and that a retired incarnation stayed a valid telemetry identity forever. Splitting the decision out, and then its mechanism after it, is the reviewers' meta-finding applied twice: a decision this size, gated — originally on AGW-17 alone, and since S5/F5 on the three gates listed below — should be ratifiable on its own rather than holding `AGW-D9` and `AGW-D13` hostage — and its machinery should be refusable without refusing the gateway.

**Rejected.** *One table keyed by `(replica_ref, incarnation)`* — a new stable id per restart, and a series split at every restart. *A character-class grammar* — it admitted `a..b`, `a.-b` and labels far past 63. *`^[a-f0-9-]{1,64}$` for the incarnation* — it admitted `-`, `--` and every other hyphen arrangement. *No caps, on the grounds that reports are trusted* — trusted means "the lease was active", not "the string is new information". *A nullable reporting-agent column so non-agent producers could register* — it would let a poller write the agent path's trust anchor; those producers need no registry at all. *Caps as prose over a `count(*)`* — round 7: two concurrent registrations at cap−1 both pass it. *A cap over the historical population* — round 7: names churn, so the cap becomes permanent exhaustion and every later replica is refused; retirement with a released slot is the fix. *Accepting a retired incarnation's labels indefinitely* — round 7: it lets a dead incarnation keep writing current samples; a registration-window check with a gap marker is the fix, and it still admits the legitimately buffered late batch.

**Ordering and gates — three of them, not one (S5/F5).** Ratifies after `BE-D*` and `AG-D*`, and only when **all** of the following close:

1. **§13 AGW-17** — runtime verification of reported replica identities, or the architect group's explicit acceptance of the grammar-bounded residual as the one `dp-remote`'s reported hostnames already carry. `AGW-D13`'s `I6` clause ratifies with it.
2. **§13 AGW-20** — the report-intake contract. This gate was **missing until S5/F5** and it is not incidental: the properties above that concern **report ordering** (the per-activation watermark, its scoping to one reporter, the stale-report rule) and everything that depends on **liveness** (`last_seen_at`, and therefore §5.0's silence retirement and the release of the stable cap's slots) consume report replay identity, freshness semantics and cross-restart ordering that this family does **not** own and that AGW-20 must settle — including the unresolved case that an ordinary reconnect changes the fencing epoch without proving a counter restart.
3. **§13 AGW-19**, for the retirement clauses specifically — silence retirement has no liveness source at all unless a healthy workload reports periodically.

**What is buildable before those close, stated precisely so it is not over-read:** the report-semantics-free portion only — the three tables' DDL, the RFC 1123 and incarnation grammars, `agent_replica_one_active`, the cap row and its arithmetic, and stable-id reuse across restarts. Admission ordering, the reporter gate's liveness fallback, silence retirement and cap release are **not**. That separation buys DDL and constraint execution, not a working registry. It is **not** ratified as part of the `AGW-D1`…`AGW-D14` set.
